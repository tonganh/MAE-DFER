The rapid rise of AI-powered tools such as chatbots, virtual therapists, and digital mental health platforms has created new opportunities to improve accessibility to mental health support. Recent evidence suggests that a substantial proportion of young people are already using AI for emotional support, with approximately 12–29% of adolescents and young adults engaging with AI tools for mental health purposes. However, growing concerns highlight significant risks associated with AI use in this domain. Studies and expert reports indicate that AI systems may misdiagnose mental health conditions, provide inappropriate advice, reinforce harmful thoughts, or fail to detect crisis situations. The review will analyse available quantitative data on the prevalence of AI use among young people, patterns of engagement, and documented outcomes both beneficial and harmful. This includes population-level trends, clinical studies, and observational data on user experiences. Overall, this project seeks to provide a balanced, critical, and evidence-informed understanding of AI in youth mental health, supporting policymakers, clinicians, educators, and developers in designing safe, effective, and equitable AI-enabled mental health solutions.
